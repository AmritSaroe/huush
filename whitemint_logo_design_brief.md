# whitemint — Logo Design Brief

## What whitemint is

**whitemint** is a private, distraction-free reading app for people who want to save important web articles and return to them with attention. A reader pastes a public article URL through a single bottom-plus action. The app retrieves the page on-device, cleans away publisher clutter, preserves article images, and stores the reading locally. It is not a social feed, a news outlet, or an algorithmic recommendation product. It is a calm personal library for articles a person has deliberately chosen.

The experience should feel like opening a well-edited personal brief rather than browsing the web. The app values **clarity over novelty, reading over scrolling, and intention over accumulation**.

| Product attribute | Design meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Private archive | Discreet, trustworthy, and personal rather than loud or performative. |
| Editorial reader | Literate, composed, typographic, and quiet. |
| Saved web articles | A sense of curation, return, and keeping what matters. |
| Mobile-first Android app | Highly legible at small sizes, immediate, touch-friendly, and robust as an app icon. |
| Focus mode | Calm, spacious, and free of ornamental UI noise. |

## Audience and emotional goal

whitemint is for curious readers who save thoughtful reporting, essays, analysis, and long-form writing from across the web. They want an alternative to browser tabs, link dumps, and attention-hungry feeds. They may save articles from publications such as business, technology, science, culture, politics, and design sites, but the brand itself should not feel tied to any one category of news.

The logo should make the user feel that their reading is **considered, safe, and worth returning to**. It should feel confident but not corporate; intelligent but not academic; modern but not tech-generic. The emotional register is closer to a small independent magazine, a beautiful notebook, or a carefully marked passage than a productivity dashboard.

> **Brand essence:** A personal reading room for the articles that deserve more time.

## Current product experience

The home screen is a clean article feed. Large editorial typography gives saved articles priority. Adding a new article happens only through a persistent circular **+** button at the bottom of the screen, which opens a focused sheet for pasting a URL. The app has light and dark themes, reading-size controls, five readable typeface choices, a center-tap focus mode that hides article controls, and a Debug section for copying diagnostic logs when an extraction issue occurs.

Article pages are intentionally spacious. They use large headlines, a source badge, real article imagery, generous line-height, and minimal controls. The brand mark will appear in small contexts such as the app header, empty library state, launcher/app icon, and possibly a subtle loading state. Therefore, the mark must work without a wordmark and remain recognizable from approximately **24 px to 1024 px**.

## Visual language of the app

The visual system is editorial, high-legibility, and restrained. It uses large type, generous whitespace, hairline rules, pale paper-like surfaces, and a vivid lime highlight that behaves like a highlighter pen over a meaningful sentence. The app deliberately avoids glossy gradients, glassmorphism, neon effects, stock productivity visuals, and excessive rounded cards.

| Element | Light theme | Dark theme | Intended feeling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary surface | Warm editorial white, approximately `#F6F5F1` | Near-black charcoal, approximately `#111113` | Paper, focus, calm. |
| Primary ink | Almost black, approximately `#121217` | Soft warm white, approximately `#F4F1E9` | Excellent readability without harshness. |
| Signature accent | Highlighter lime, approximately `#C6FA55` | Softer lime, approximately `#B2DD48` | A deliberate mark of attention. |
| Supporting color | Muted gray typography and rules | Warm gray typography and rules | Quiet hierarchy. |
| Typography | Large sans-serif interface text with an editorial serif for headlines | The same system, with softened contrast | Reading first, interface second. |

The logo should look at home on both the warm-white light theme and charcoal dark theme. It should remain strong in one color and should not depend on a background color to be understandable.

## Recommended logo direction

Create a **symbol-first identity**. The primary deliverable should be an icon or mark without the word “whitemint.” A wordmark can be explored separately, but the app needs a symbol that works as an Android launcher icon and inside compact UI locations.

The symbol should express the idea of **a page worth keeping**. The best interpretation is abstract rather than literal. Possible visual ingredients include a folded editorial page, a marked passage, a quiet open spread, a corner turn, a reading window, or a simple typographic gesture that suggests a saved thought. If using a book or page idea, simplify it aggressively so it does not become a generic bookstore, education app, or e-reader icon.

A small lime component is welcome, but it should function as a precise accent—like the highlighter in the interface—not as a large decorative field. The symbol should have a strong black/white version first, then a version with the lime accent.

## Required characteristics

| Requirement | What it means in practice |
| --- | --- |
| Simple silhouette | The mark should be identifiable in a single glance and survive favicon-scale reduction. |
| Editorial rather than corporate | Prefer the feeling of a refined magazine or reading room over a fintech, SaaS, or AI company. |
| Original | Avoid copying recognizable marks from publishers, reading apps, note-taking products, or book retailers. |
| Calm geometry | Use disciplined proportions, limited strokes, and intentional negative space. |
| Theme-ready | Work on light and dark backgrounds, ideally as pure ink, reversed ink, and ink-plus-lime. |
| App-icon ready | Fit naturally within a rounded-square Android icon container without needing a detailed scene. |
| No typography dependency | The primary symbol must work with no text at all. |

## Avoid

Avoid the following directions because they would dilute the product’s personality:

| Avoid | Why it does not fit |
| --- | --- |
| Generic open book, graduation cap, or library building | Makes the app feel educational, institutional, or literal. |
| Bookmark ribbon as the main mark | Too common for reading and save-for-later apps. |
| Brain, lightbulb, rocket, sparkles, or AI motif | Makes the app feel like a generic productivity or AI product. |
| Speech bubble, RSS wave, newspaper masthead, or globe | Suggests publishing, social news, or a news aggregator rather than a private archive. |
| Leaves, mint sprigs, or literal plants | The name is metaphorical; a plant would be misleading and decorative. |
| Gradients, 3D effects, thin intricate details, or shadows | They reduce small-size clarity and conflict with the editorial interface. |
| Overly rounded, playful, or childlike geometry | The app is warm but mature and serious about reading. |
| A logo that requires the wordmark to make sense | It will fail in compact app locations. |

## Desired personality

The mark should feel **literate, private, precise, warm, modern, and quietly confident**. It should not feel luxurious in a flashy way, nor aggressively minimal in a cold way. Think of an object someone would trust to hold their best reading: a carefully designed bookplate, a marked page, or a small editorial seal.

If the logo has movement, it should suggest a gentle **turn, reveal, or kept fragment**, not speed, disruption, or automation.

## Ready-to-use prompt for a logo designer or Claude

> Design a modern, scalable symbol-first logo for **whitemint**, a private mobile app for saving web articles and reading them later without distractions. The app feels like a personal reading room and editorial archive, not a social feed or news publisher. Its visual style is high-legibility and editorial: pale paper surfaces, near-black ink, generous whitespace, large type, hairline rules, and one highlighter-lime accent (`#C6FA55`). In dark mode it uses charcoal black with a softer lime accent.
>
> Create an original, minimalist mark that suggests “a page worth keeping” or “a reading moment preserved.” Explore abstract cues such as a folded page, marked passage, quiet open spread, corner turn, reading window, or restrained editorial seal. Make the concept symbolic rather than literal. The primary mark must work without text at 24 px, as an Android launcher icon, in a small app header, and at large sizes. It should look strong in one color, reversed in white, and with a small precise lime accent. Use calm geometry, confident negative space, and a simple recognizable silhouette.
>
> Avoid generic open-book illustrations, bookmark ribbons, newspaper motifs, RSS, chat bubbles, AI sparkles, plants or mint leaves, lightbulbs, gradients, 3D effects, shadows, and detailed linework. Do not include a wordmark in the primary symbol. The result should feel literate, private, composed, and quietly modern—like a refined independent magazine or personal bookplate rather than a SaaS dashboard.
